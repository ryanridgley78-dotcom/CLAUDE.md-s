YM YHWH's Misfits

Jared Cheshire Paper

The Sacred Order of Love

A written teaching from Jared Cheshire.

Headship and Authority According to Scripture

Abstract

This paper explores the foundational structure of biblical love as a dynamic, hierarchical relationship rooted in the sacred flow of authority from YHWH to man, and from man to woman. By analyzing the vertical and horizontal expressions of love outlined in the Scriptures, this study articulates a divine order in which provision, protection, and affection flow downward through legitimate headship, while reverence, obedience, and submission ascend as responsive currents. Drawing from both the Tanakh and the Messianic Writings, the study seeks to establish a biblically faithful model of relational harmony that transcends modern egalitarian constructs without promoting abuse or subjugation. The argument focuses solely on headship as the essential structure upon which all other relational constructs must rest.

By Jared G. Cheshire

Introduction

What is love?

It’s a question that has echoed through the corridors of human history, from ancient philosophers to modern pop culture. In the 1990s, the song What Is Love by Haddaway gave voice to this timeless question, turning it into an anthem of Generation X. With its haunting refrain “Baby don’t hurt me, no more,” the song captures a deep and often unspoken fear: that love, misunderstood and misapplied, becomes the very source of pain it is supposed to heal. This confusion arises from a culture that has severed love from its divine framework. Stripped of order and structure, “love” becomes subjective, emotional, and ultimately destructive.

But Scripture tells a different story. According to the Word of YHWH, love is not merely a feeling or a fleeting emotion. Rather, it is a covenantal action that flows through divinely established authority. When we lose sight of this structure, we lose sight of what love actually is. In both the Tanakh and the Messianic Writings, love is not free-floating sentiment but a pattern of relational responsibility governed by headship and submission. It begins with YHWH, flows through His appointed order, and returns in the form of obedience and reverence. This sacred exchange forms the basis of all true relationships, whether between YHWH and man, man and woman, or between neighbors and brethren.

The purpose of this paper is to define the structure of relational headship as ordained by YHWH, and to reassert that headship is not oppressive, it is protective. The scriptural model of authority was never designed to dominate but to serve, to provide, and to guide through self sacrifice. Yet in our modern world, where egalitarianism has distorted roles and flattened distinctions, this divine model has been maligned as tyranny, dismissed as old fashioned, and patriarchy mocked as the enemy of modern society. The result has been relational chaos, marital breakdown, and spiritual disconnection. To restore order, we must return to the blueprint, not the blueprint of culture, but the blueprint of Scripture.

This study will approach the subject of headship through both exegetical and thematic lenses. First, we will examine specific passages that illustrate the downward flow of love from YHWH to man, and the upward response of reverent obedience. Then we will explore how this vertical order is mirrored in the human domain, particularly in the relationship between man and woman. Finally, we will consider how horizontal relationships among peers are only sustainable when grounded in the vertical hierarchy established by the Most High.

What emerges is a sacred pattern: love flows down from authority as provision, protection, and affection, and love flows up through submission, obedience, and reverence. This is neither mutual independence, nor desperate codependence, but divine interdependence. In this design, the Most High is not only the origin of love but also the architect of its structure. To participate in love is to submit to that structure. Any attempt to bypass or rewrite it results in a counterfeit that may resemble love in imagery but will fail in substance.

In a world where “love” is shouted from stages of entertainment and religiosity, rarely is it lived out in covenant. We must ask the question again, but this time let us look to the source of all truth. What is love? The answer begins with YHWH and flows through the headship He has ordained.

The Vertical Design of Love: From YHWH to Man

The nature of love is not born from the will or imagination of humanity. It begins above. Every authentic expression of love is first modeled by YHWH Himself in His dealings with mankind. Before man can love rightly, whether toward woman or neighbor, he must first receive and reflect the divine love that flows down from heaven. That love is structured, intentional, and never aimless. It manifests in three foundational expressions: provision, protection, and affection. Each of these is a demonstration of headship. YHWH does not ask anything of man that He has not first exemplified through His own covering relationship toward us.

YHWH as Provider

Genesis 22:14 – “YHWH Yireh”

The revelation of YHWH as provider (YHWH Yireh) comes at one of the most intense moments of obedience in Scripture. Abraham is prepared to offer up his promised son Isaac in obedience to the Most High. In that moment of surrender, YHWH reveals a substitute sacrifice and establishes a truth that would echo through generations: provision follows submission. The name YHWH Yireh, “YHWH will provide,” is not a generic promise of abundance; it is the title of a sovereign King Whose character it is to provide for those under His authority. This act of provision is not merely material, it is deeply relational. YHWH provides within covenant. His supply is not random generosity but structured love. The head provides because the subordinate obeys.

Psalm 23:1 – “Ishall not want”

David, a king himself under the authority of YHWH, declares:

“YHWH is my shepherd, I shall not want.”

In this, we see the ultimate model of what it means to be ruled by divine headship. Provision is not about wealth; it is about sufficiency. When one walks under the authority of YHWH, want disappears, not necessarily because abundance overflows, but because peace reigns. Provision, in YHWH’s design, is inseparably tied to the presence of right order. The shepherd provides because the sheep follow. Rebellion disrupts provision, but submission ensures sufficiency. This is not poetic imagery, it is a blueprint for relational function.

YHWH as Protector

Psalm 91:4 – Under His wings

Protection is love in action, especially when authority shields the vulnerable. Psalm 91 paints a vivid picture of YHWH as a protective head.

“He shall cover you with His feathers, and under His wings you shall take refuge.”

This protective imagery of a hen with chicks is not weakness, it is allegory for covenantal covering, willingness to fight and die to ensure the protected continues to live. Like a sovereign warrior who simultaneously nurtures and defends, YHWH provides the security that no earthly protector can offer. His authority becomes a barrier against chaos. Submission to this covering invites refuge; rebellion rejects it. The love of YHWH protects, but only those who dwell in His order, under His wings.

Isaiah 41:10 – Strength and help

“Do not fear, for I am with you… I will strengthen you, surely I will help you.”

These words, spoken by the Most High through the prophet Isaiah, show that divine authority is not distant but very near, very present. Headship is not simply an impersonal position; it is active involvement. YHWH’s protection includes emotional and spiritual security, strength in weakness, and help in trouble on a personal level. He promises presence, not just power, personal interaction, not just oversight. This is not the authoritarian image painted by critics of biblical structure. This is headship defined by nearness and reliability, by involvement and intimacy. Authority protects; not with mere surveillance, but with strength sacrificially poured out on behalf of the one under covering. It is a giving of Himself, a cost to Himself,that He gives willingly for the betterment of the one He is covering.

Exodus 14:14 – YHWH will fight for you

The children of Israel stood terrified before the Red Sea, pressed by Pharaoh’s army. In that moment, Moses declared a truth central to headship:

“YHWH will fight for you; you need only to be still.”

This was not passivity, but trust. YHWH, as the Bridegroom King, had taken the children of Jacob as His nation, His betrothed brides. Headship demands not only provision and guidance, but warfare on behalf of the covered. Love here is manifested in deliverance. He does not watch His people be overrun, He intervenes. His authority is not abstract but militant in protection. True biblical love never abandons; it stands between the threat and the beloved.

In each of these examples, YHWH’s love is not a concept but a function. It manifests through responsibility. He takes it upon Himself to secure the safety of those who walk in covenant with Him. This is not only the model of divine love, but the foundation of masculine love, which is called to reflect Him in the world.

YHWH as Affectionate Authority

Zephaniah 3:17 – Rejoices with singing

Authority and affection are often seen as incompatible in modern thinking, but the Most High embodies both simultaneously.

“YHWH your Elohim is in your midst, a mighty one who will save; He will rejoice over you with gladness… He will exult over you with loud singing.”

This is not weakness, it is strength expressed as joy. YHWH does not simply tolerate His people, He delights in them. His authority does not extinguish emotional expression; it sanctifies it. This is not indulgent love that requires nothing in return, but covenantal affection based on obedience and nearness. He rejoices because the relationship is rightly ordered.

Deuteronomy 7:9 – Covenant-keeping faithfulness

“Know therefore that YHWH your Elohim is Elohim, the faithful Elohim, who keeps covenant and steadfast love with those who love Him and keep His commandments.”

This verse affirms that affection and consistency are intertwined in divine love. YHWH’s love is loyal. He does not flinch at hardship, nor abandon when His people stumble. Yet His affection is not unconditional in the modern sense. It is loyal to those who keep His commands. His enduring love is directed toward those who respect His authority. In this, affection becomes a reward of relational order, not a blanket indulgence given apart from obedience.

This expression of love reminds us that true authority does not distance itself emotionally. It leans in. YHWH’s affection is holy, purposeful, and directed. When human headship reflects this, affection is neither manipulation nor neglect, it is the overflow of righteous responsibility.

Communion through Revelation

Psalm 25:14 – Intimacy reserved for those who fear Him

“The secret of YHWH is with those who fear Him, and He will show them His covenant.”

This passage reveals a profound truth: intimacy with the Most High is not for everyone, it is for those who revere Him. The word translated “fear” implies awe, reverence, and voluntary submission. The secrets of YHWH, His covenantal truths, His guidance, His plans, are not cast before the disobedient or irreverent (Matthew 7:6). He reveals Himself intimately only to those who honor His authority and submit to His ways.

This is the relational peak of divine love. Not only does YHWH provide, protect, and rejoice over His people, He shares His heart with them. However, this intimacy has a condition: reverence. The man who fears YHWH is invited into deeper revelation. That revelation is not just information, it is communion. In the same manner that a husband shares the inner workings of his soul with a wife who respects him, so too does YHWH share with those who walk in submissive awe. That reverence is the source of all wisdom according to Psalm 111:10:

“The fear of YHWH is the beginning of wisdom; A good understanding have all those who do His commandments; His praise endures forever.”

(Also refer to Proverbs 1:7, Proverbs 9:10, and 2 Kings 17:25)

The Ascending Response: From Man to YHWH

Love does not merely descend, it also ascends but the method is different. As love flows downward from YHWH in the form of provision, protection, and affection, man is not meant to remain a passive recipient. The response expected from those under divine headship is obedience rooted in love, reverence, and submission. This is the upward current that completes the sacred circuit. Love is never a one-way transaction; it is covenantal. And in covenant, both parties act. YHWH acts with righteous authority; man responds with faithful submission to His authority. When this response is absent, love is interrupted. It remains unfulfilled. But when man answers the divine with submission, the relationship becomes whole.

Think of the circuit as blood flow. Love is pumped out from the heart via high pressure arteries (authority), it flows out to every member of the body (household), it's then returned upward through vessels with check valves to keep it from flowing back down between beats, it's still pressurized but at a much more relaxed pressure because the heart still bears the responsibility for the initiation of the flow. If there is an interruption in that flow, a bleed (apathy), a clot (resistance), inflammation (disobedience) in a member, there are major medical (relational) ramifications that, if not corrected, could result in death (severed relationship).

Obedience as Love

Deuteronomy 6:5 – With all your heart, soul, and strength

This command, given to Israel at Sinai, is not abstract. It sets the tone for the entire covenant:

“You shall love YHWH your Elohim with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your strength.”

This is not an emotional suggestion, rather it is a demand for alignment. In the Hebrew understanding, love is not a feeling one waits to feel; it is a posture of loyalty, service, and total life orientation toward YHWH. To love Him with all your heart is to conform your character and will to His way. To love Him with all your soul is to intentionally give all your mental focus on His mission. To love Him with all your strength is to spend every ounce of effort in obedience.

This command bridges the vertical relationship between YHWH and man. It reminds the people that YHWH’s provision and protection are not unconditional in the sense of modern popular religion. They are covenantal. To be under His wings requires remaining within His order. Loving YHWH is therefore not sentimental—it is practical. It is lived out through the mind, will, and physical life of the believer. It is love as loyalty, expressed through submission, obedience, and adoration to your covenantal Head.

John 14:15 – “If you love Me, keep My commandments”

These words spoken by the Messiah remove all ambiguity. He does not separate love from law, nor grace from obedience. He does not ask for affection without submission.

“If you love Me, keep My commandments.”

The condition is clear. Obedience is the evidence of love. This is the ultimate test of sincerity. Many may claim love with their lips, but Yeshua demands it with action. He does not seek admiration, He requires allegiance.

In context, this statement comes during a conversation about comfort, peace, and the giving of the Spirit. That Spirit, however, is not given to rebels. It is given to those who obey. Love is not a matter of the mouth but of the walk. Yeshua, reflecting the Father’s authority, insists on obedience as the defining marker of relational love. True discipleship is not merely emotional or theological, it is behavioral.

1 John 5:3 – His commandments are not burdensome

The apostle John, decades later, would reaffirm what his Master taught.

“For this is the love of Elohim, that we keep His commandments; and His commandments are not burdensome.”

Here, love is defined again by obedience. And not just obedience, but joyful, willing obedience (a principal found in 2 Corinthians 9:7). The commandments of YHWH are not a heavy yoke, they are the structure within which covenantal love flourishes.

This verse not only corrects the false claim that love nullifies His instructions (Torah), but it also rebukes the idea that obedience is oppressive. Burdensome commandments are those imposed by tyrants. But YHWH’s commands are protective boundaries. They are not unbearable weights but walls that keep the covenant people safe within His covering. Love, then, is not crushed under obedience, it thrives in it. The man who loves YHWH keeps His commands, not because he must, but because he desires to stay within the presence and favor of his Head.

Reverence and Submission

Proverbs 1:7 – Fear as the beginning of knowledge

“The fear of YHWH is the beginning of knowledge; fools despise wisdom and instruction.”

Love cannot rise if reverence does not first take root. This “fear” is not dread, it is awe, honor, and a holy caution that refuses to treat YHWH casually. To revere Him is to recognize His position as Head over all creation and to live accordingly. It is to honor His commands as supreme wisdom.

Without reverence, knowledge becomes corrupted, twisted by self-will and human pride. But with reverence, wisdom begins to flow. The man who fears YHWH walks in humility, guarding his steps, eager to please. Reverence creates the posture that invites deeper instruction. It is not terror, but neither is it flippancy. It is the heart that says, “I do not want to grieve the One who covers me.” This is the fear that leads to obedience, not to earn favor, but to maintain the closeness He first extended.

Genesis 22: Abraham’s obedience through tested trust

Nowhere is this fear, trust, and submission more evident than in Abraham’s obedience in Genesis 22. When YHWH commanded him to offer Isaac, the promised son, Abraham obeyed without hesitation. He did not bargain. He did not delay. He did not demand explanation. He simply trusted the authority of the One who gave the command, believing that YHWH was able to raise the dead if necessary (Hebrews 11:19).

This obedience was not empty action, it was a declaration of loyalty, faith, and reverence. YHWH Himself affirms this when He says,

“Now I know that you fear Elohim.”

Abraham’s love for YHWH was proven not by feeling, but by action, by surrender. This is the kind of obedience that reveals true headship. Abraham did not obey out of compulsion, but out of trust. He knew the nature of YHWH’s love and provision, and thus he submitted even when it hurt, having faith in the promise.

This is the model of reverent submission: tested obedience that flows from love and trust. In this moment, Abraham teaches us that love for YHWH may cost us everything, but will never leave us without fulfillment. Reverence is not passivity. It is strength restrained in surrender to One greater.

Completing the Circuit of Love

Obedience as the means by which YHWH’s love is fully manifest

YHWH’s love flows downward in perfection. It is never lacking, never inattentive, never arbitrary in its origin. It is manifestation, it is a realized benefit in the life of man, it is activated through obedience. Just as electricity completes a circuit only when connected, love completes its purpose only when returned in obedience and submission. We are the switch which completes the circuit of divine love.

The vertical design of love is covenantal. YHWH, as the righteous Head, initiates and sustains the covenant. However, He does not force His covering upon the disobedient. The blessings of His provision, protection, and affection are accessed through alignment. When a man rebels, he steps outside of that flow. The love remains constant in nature, but its effects are withheld by design. Think back to the blood flow analogy.

Obedience is not transactional, it is relational. It does not purchase love, but it positions the heart to receive it. Like a wife who receives the covering of her husband when she trusts and honors his leadership, so too does man receive the full benefit of YHWH’s love when he walks in submission. The circuit is completed, not when man sings songs or speaks words, but when he lives in accordance with the authority of the Most High.

This is how love ascends: not in noise or ritual, but in ordered life, reverent fear, and faithful obedience.

Headship Among Humans

Man as Image-Bearer of Authority

The vertical structure of love does not terminate with man. YHWH has designed man to reflect His image, not only in character, but in functional headship. Just as YHWH provides, protects, and pours out covenantal affection upon those who submit to Him, man is expected to mirror these attributes within his own domain. The household, therefore, becomes the sacred theater in which the saga of divine love plays out on earth. In this framework, man is not a dictator but a delegate, an appointed steward entrusted to reflect the righteous rulership of the Most High. The woman, made from his side, is not a rival for that headship, but a responder to it. She is designed to reflect the reverent submission that mankind offers back to YHWH. The harmony of the home is directly tied to alignment with this heavenly pattern.

1 Corinthians 11:3, The Hierarchy: YHWH→Messiah→Man→Woman

“But I want you to understand that Messiah is the head of every man, and the man is the head of a woman, and Elohim is the head of Messiah.”

In this one verse, the apostle Paul lays out the divine order. It is not a manmade hierarchy, nor is it cultural or temporal. It is rooted in eternity. YHWH (Spirit) is the Head of Messiah (physical manifestation), who is the Head of man (image of his Elohim), who is the head of woman (image of her man). This is the circuit of sacred love manifest in functional roles. Each link in the chain reflects the one above it and serves the one beneath. There is no redundancy, no rivalry, no reversal, and no insertion of an outside party into the structure.

This passage does not teach intrinsic worth of individual parties, but their function in the system. It is not about superiority, but structure. Even Messiah, referred to propheticly as the arm of Elohim, is equal with the Spirit, the Father, in essence, operates in submission to His Head. So too must man submit to Messiah, and woman submit to man, not in oppression, but in order. This flow of headship is not burdensome, it is the bloodstream of harmony, designed to pump life, provision, and peace into every home that will embrace it.

Man’s Role as Representative Head

Authority to provide, protect, and love

Man’s role as head is not self-appointed, it is a calling. He is tasked with the same threefold responsibilities that YHWH demonstrates toward him: to provide, to protect, and to be intimate. Provision is not merely material, it is also spiritual leadership, emotional strength, and mental stability. Just as YHWH is our Shepherd, man must shepherd his household, ensuring that none under his covering live in lack, especially lack of guidance, and yes, correction.

Protection mirrors divine intervention. Man must stand in the gap against threats both physical and spiritual, just as YHWH stood between Israel and Pharaoh with his false deities. He is to shield, to guard, and to comfort. This is not passive existence but active guardianship. He must lay himself down; his comfort, his preferences, even his safety for the sake of those under his charge. Headship is not a platform for dominance, but a posture of sacrificial strength.

Finally, he must intimately love, not with indulgence, but with covenantal affection. Like YHWH rejoicing over His people, a man must delight in his woman, not as a trinket or trophy but as a precious possession to responsibly steward. His affection must be holy, targeted, and never manipulative. Just as YHWH’s affection is grounded in provisional fidelity, so too must a man’s provision and protection of his love be consistent, trustworthy, and righteous.

Reflecting the attributes of YHWH’s stewardship

Man was created to be an image-bearer. His rulership on earth is not autonomous, it is representative. He is to model YHWH’s stewardship by embodying righteous authority: strong yet gentle, firm yet fair, protective yet personal. When he leads with wisdom and love, he reflects the order of the heavens within the walls of his home.

His authority is not license, it is stewardship. He is held accountable, just as Adam was when he failed to lead in Eden. Man must answer for the condition of those under his covering. If he is careless, the flow of love breaks down. But if he reflects YHWH’s leadership faithfully, his home will become a sanctuary of order, honor, and shalom to every household member.

The Role of the Woman

Ephesians 5:22–24 – Submission as alignment to the husband’s mission

“Wives, submit to your own husbands as to the Master. For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Messiah is the head of the assembly, His body, and is Himself its Savior.”

This submission is not blind servitude, it is sacred alignment. Just as man must submit to Messiah’s mission, so too must woman align herself with her husband’s purpose. She is not just following a man, she is following the mission he has been given from above in the righteous chain of command.

Submission is trust in motion. It is the yielding of preference, not value. The wife does not forfeit her identity, she fulfills it by bringing her strength under voluntary control, assimilating her identity into the identity of her husband just as Messiah submitted to the will of the Father and was completely One in Him. Her submission is her worship, not to a mere man, but to YHWH, through the structure He has ordained as His Image of Himself to her. Tangentially, her self-assertion of her own will in contrast to his will is not expression of her identity, but the perversion of it. The text of verse 24 says “submit in everything,” not selectively, not occasionally, nor when she approves or agrees. Her covenant is to follow her head, just as he follows his. Furthermore, I have heard it said, that if you agree, it's not submission because you would do it anyway. Submission implies that it's something you would not have done of your own accord.

When she does this, she becomes a conduit of peace in the home. Her presence affirms his leadership. Her support strengthens his resolve. Her willingness to trust allows him to flourish in his calling. The house thrives because its flow is unbroken.

1 Peter 3:1–6 – Quiet spirit and reverence

Peter gives deeper insight into the posture of submission:

“In the same way, you wives, be submissive to your own husbands… let it be the hidden person of the heart, with the imperishable quality of a gentle and quiet spirit, which is precious in the sight of Elohim.”

Here, submission is described as something deeper than action, it is attitude. The reverent woman does not need to dominate, demand, or demean. Her strength is in restraint. Her influence is silent but seismic.

Peter links this attitude to the holy women of old; women like Sarah, who called Abraham “master.” This was not because Abraham was perfect, but because Sarah trusted the Elohim who appointed him. A wife’s reverence is a direct mirror of mankind’s fear of YHWH. She does not submit because she thinks he is always right, but because she honors the structure YHWH has ordained, especially when she doesn't. Her gentle spirit becomes a dwelling place for the presence of the Most High.

Fulfillment Within Distinct Roles

Headship is responsibility, not entitlement

The authority given to man is never for his benefit alone, in-fact it is a weighty assignment. He does not receive honor without burden, nor reverence without responsibility. Headship is the calling to lay down one’s life; daily, sacrificially, purposefully. To lead is to die to self. Just as YHWH bears the responsibility of provision, protection, and affection, so too must the man bear the burden of care for his household.

If he neglects this, he damages his Master’s authority in his home. A man who dominates is not a head, he is a tyrant imagining not YHWH, but the enemy. However, a man who leads through righteous stewardship becomes a channel through which the love of YHWH flows. His strength is not for dictatorial ownership but for sacrificial service as the pilot holding the rudder of his ship, staring it away from Rocky shoals and navigating through challenging straights. His leadership is not for glory but for order. In this sacred posture, he becomes a living reflection of the Father above.

Submission is trust, not inferiority

Submission is one of the most misunderstood concepts in modern culture. It is often equated with weakness or subjugation, but Scripture presents it as a strength rooted in trust. A woman who submits is not weak, she is amazingly strong, strong enough to subject her unrighteous desire to rule to the headship of her husband (Genesis 3:16). She sees the covering YHWH has placed over her and chooses to honor it against her fleshly nature. Her submission is a spiritual act, a form of worship to YHWH that upholds His divine order. What the world views as a strong independent woman is actually a sign of spiritual weakness. It is a woman who was too weak to resist and subjugate her fallen nature.

She is not less valuable to the Kingdom economy than her husband, she is a different currency than he. She was not formed from his head to rule over him, nor from the soles of his feet to be trampled, but from his side near his heart and breath, to embrace his spirit and thrive under his leadership. Her value is not diminished by her submission, it is revealed by it. Just as man must submit to Messiah, taking on His identity and calling, so too does the woman reveal her glory under the banner of reverent obedience.

When both roles are embraced, when the man leads with sacrificial love and the woman responds with joyful submission, the home becomes a mirror of the celestial. The circuit of love is completed. The headship of YHWH is honored. And His image is made visible in the household He designed.

Interdependence Within the Sacred Order

The structure of love, headship, and submission is not about isolation of roles but about their interdependence. Just as blood cannot flow to the body without first being pumped from the heart, love cannot rise without first descending. And just as blood must return through the veins to complete the circuit, submission must rise in response to righteous authority. This sacred order is a system of mutual accountability: one initiates in righteousness, the other responds in faithfulness. When both the authoritative and the submissive parties perform their respective role, the entire household lives. When either neglects their part, the entire structure suffers loss. This is not a hierarchy for domineering control, it is a divine ecosystem of function, flow, and fruitfulness.

Downward Love Calls for Upward Obedience

Love must flow down before submission can flow up

Submission is not spontaneous, it is responsive to an opportunity (grace). Just as the bloodstream requires the initial force of the heart to activate the circulation, so too must love be initiated by the head. The man, as head, must love first; not because the woman deserves it, but because he is appointed to represent YHWH’s initiating role. It is the proposal of reciprocal love. A woman cannot truly thrive in reverent submission to a man who refuses to lead in righteousness. Headship begins not with demands, but with an offer and opportunity. YHWH does not ask man to obey first before offering a proposal of expectations that will show Himself faithful through provision, protection, and affection. Likewise, the man must establish the flow of love through an offer of righteous expectations before he expects submission to rise from a woman.

The home stagnates when love is withheld at the top. Just as blood pools and becomes toxic when the heart fails to beat properly, so too does relational dysfunction spread when the one in authority withholds the love of direction. The wife cannot respect what does not first reveal the image of YHWH. Therefore, the man must be the first mover in love, just as YHWH was the first mover in our salvation, through the grace of the offer to come into Covenant with Him.

“We love Him because He first loved us.” (1 John 4:19)

Authority properly functions only when submission responds faithfully

However, just as love must initiate from above, the system still fails if submission does not respond below. A man can love, lead, and lay down his life, but if the woman resists, refuses, or rebels, the circuit breaks. The household becomes frustrated, and the image of YHWH is dishonored. Even YHWH does not override rebellion, He withdraws when submission is absent. In the same way, a man cannot fully operate in headship when the one under his covering will not receive it.

Submission activates the authority’s function. The man’s love has no target if a woman will not receive it and return it. Her submission is what transforms his leadership from potential to power. She gives his love a place to land. Without her reverent response, he may still walk in righteousness before YHWH, offering his provision, and protection, but the circuit of relational blessing in the home remains incomplete and his abilities to render affection and intimacy is negatively impacted, if not outright killed. The flow of authority and love is mutual, but not symmetrical, each must move according to design.

Protection and Provision in Practice

The woman thrives under secure headship

A woman is not called to thrive in chaos or uncovered exposure of singleness. Her role, while strong, is not intended to be self-sustaining. Just as mankind requires YHWH’s covering to function properly, a woman requires the belonging to the secure headship of her husband to flourish in her feminine design. The modern narrative praises independence, but Scripture calls it a reproach and in turn esteems alignment to a righteous man. A woman finds peace when she no longer seeks to carry the burden of leadership that was never hers to bear. Her strength is not erased, it is directed and magnified by him.

Secure headship provides not only material provision but spiritual protection and emotional clarity. When a man leads as YHWH commands, the woman walks at liberty in her role as his helper. She no longer needs to assert, control, or defend her position. Her soul finds stillness because trust is no longer viewed as a risk, it is rightly understood as a reward. This peace allows her spirit to bloom, her heart to soften, and her influence to deepen. Like a garden under the sun, she produces fruit when the climate is rightly ordered.

Man thrives in YHWH’s covering when he walks in obedience

Likewise, the man does not operate autonomously. He is also under headship. Just as the woman responds to his leadership, he must respond to Messiah’s. His household authority is only legitimate if it mirrors the submission he himself practices. He cannot demand obedience from others while walking in rebellion to YHWH. If he desires covering, he must remain under it.

A man who walks in disobedience forfeits the very protection and provision he is called to reflect. Like Adam, who failed to guard and govern in Eden, he invites disorder when he abandons obedience. But when he submits to YHWH, he is supplied with wisdom, strength, and discernment to lead well. His prayers are heard, his steps are directed, and his house is shielded; not by his own strength, but by the strength of his Head. Just as the woman thrives under secure headship, so too does the man thrive when he abides under Messiah’s authority.

Respect and Reverence in Human Order

Ephesians 5:33 – Wife must revere her husband

“Let each one of you in particular so love his own wife as himself, and let the wife see that she reverence her husband.”

The word “reverence” here (Greek: phobeō) is the same root used for fear or deep respect. It is not casual admiration, it is holy esteem. It is awe for the role he has been given, the power he wields, and the burden he carries. In Scripture, this level of respect is never directed to mere men, it is reserved for those who carry sacred authority. That’s why the only legitimate man to be called “Reverend” is a husband, and only by the mouth of a submitted woman placed under his headship. And he is Reverend only insofar as he reflects the ultimate Reverend: YHWH Himself.

This reverence does not originate from charm, charisma, or competence. It comes from alignment. It is the byproduct of honoring the structure YHWH has ordained. A woman who reveres her husband gives weight to his words, consideration to his leadership, and priority to his direction. She does these things willfully in her own mind, with her own hands. In doing so, she dignifies the covenant and invites blessing into her home: by choice not compulsion. Make no mistake, it is a choice that must be made, otherwise the system that is meant to circulate will bleed out, she will become inflamed with self-righteousness, his heart will quit beating towards her, and if not corrected there will be an amputation or a spiritual and psychological death. Keep in mind the wisdom elucidated in Proverbs 14:1,

“The wise woman builds her [husband's] house, But the foolish tears it down with her own hands.”

The word for house in Hebrew is masculine, the context is that the house that she builds or tears down belongs to the man. She must realize that regardless of which action she takes, she is inside of it, to either dwell in safety or be crushed under the rubble.

Mirrored in man's reverence for YHWH

This reverence is not one-sided. It reflects the same awe a man is commanded to offer YHWH. The fear of YHWH is the beginning of wisdom, and the fear of YHWH is what makes a man fit to lead. If a man wants to be reverenced, he must first become a reverent man. His obedience, humility, and submission to the Most High sets the standard. He becomes trustworthy because he himself trusts, and his works validates that faith.

Just as a woman cannot thrive unless submitted to a man, the man who rebels against YHWH cannot rightly expect reverence if he does not walk in reverence. The headship structure is vertical in flow, but circular in dependence. The man who kneels before YHWH will stand with strength before his family. The woman who honors her husband’s headship will become a testimony to angels and community alike. Most importantly her children will call her blessed, and her husband's heart faithfully trusts her to do the right thing.

This is interdependence: not weakness, but wisdom. Not restriction, but reward. It is the circuit of divine love made visible through ordered relationships. The home becomes holy ground when each member operates in their rightful place, honoring the sacred flow established from the foundation of creation.

The Horizontal Overflow

Love Between Peers

Love, when rightly aligned vertically, must overflow horizontally to our neighbor who shares the same position in the hierarchy of the divine authority structure. The righteous order between YHWH, Messiah, man, and woman does not end with the household, it expands outward into the covenant community. Every relationship in the assembly is a reflection of this divine structure. The way we treat our brothers and sisters in Messiah reveals the authenticity of our love for our Head. It is not enough to simply say we love YHWH; that love must be visible in how we relate to others who are also under His authority. Like blood from the heart that reaches every limb and extremity, the love we receive from above must reach outward to sustain and strengthen the broader body.

Leviticus 19:18 / Matthew 22:39 – Loving One's Neighbor as Oneself

“You shall love your neighbor as yourself: I am YHWH.” (Leviticus 19:18)

Yeshua echoed this in Matthew 22:39, declaring it the second greatest commandment (the header over the second five commandments) after loving YHWH (the header over the first five commandments). This is not a call to emotional niceness, it is a summons to covenant loyalty extended outward. Just as man responds to YHWH with submission, he also demonstrates that loyalty by honoring others in the household of faith. To love a neighbor rightly, one must first love YHWH rightly. This is why the command to love your neighbor is never first, it is always second. Horizontal love is born of vertical submission.

This “neighbor” is not just anyone, it is your fellow covenant-keeper, one who is under the same Head. To love them is to serve them, prefer them over those not in the household, forgive them, and assist them when they falter. It includes seeking the lost sheep, pursuing restoration, and welcoming the repentant. It is a mission of alignment; bringing others into the household, into right order, and under the covering of Messiah. True neighborly love is Kingdom love; structured, sacrificial, and submissive to YHWH’s design. We love our neighbor because we love the Kingdom, we love the Kingdom because we love the King.

Brotherhood and Sisterhood Within the Assembly

The family of faith is a mirror of the household. Brotherhood is not mere camaraderie, it is co-labor under the same authority. Brothers are fellow servants, co-workers in the field of the Master, each with a distinct call but equal in accountability to Messiah. Sisterhood is likewise not competition or comparison, it is mutual support in reverent submission to the design of YHWH. Within this holy community, each member has a role that reflects the order above.

Paul reinforces this structure when he says,

“Love does no harm to a neighbor; therefore love is the fulfillment of the law” (Romans 13:10),

and

“All the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this: ‘You shall love your neighbor as yourself’” (Galatians 5:14).

James calls this “the royal law,” urging the assembly to honor it without favoritism (James 2:8). These commands reflect horizontal alignment, but they assume that vertical order is already in place.

In brotherhood, men walk side by side, not as equals in function, but as equals in calling, each under the Headship of Messiah. Brotherhood is strengthened not by shared opinions, but by shared submission. And that submission births cooperation, patience, and forgiveness. Rebuke flows from love like a liver corrects the malfunctioning pancreas, both sharing the same flow of blood. Accountability is embraced, not avoided. The righteous man does not cover sin in his brother’s life; he confronts it, prays for him, and helps him back into alignment. Brotherhood protects holiness by protecting order, helping him to bear his burden so that he can come back into alignment with submission to their shared Master.

In sisterhood, women serve each other not by empowering rebellion but by reinforcing alignment. Titus 2:3–5 commands older women to teach the younger; not abstract empowerment, but concrete submission: to love their husbands and children, to be self-controlled, pure, working at home, kind, and submissive to their own husbands. This is not cultural oppression, it is divine structure. A wise woman builds up other women not by leading them away from headship, but by calling them back under it by being a righteous example.

1 Timothy 5:16 describes the necessity of the household functioning with clarity, where widows are cared for within the household structure, not left to fend for themselves or become dependent on the assembly unnecessarily. These instructions recognize that the body functions when order is preserved. Just as there is a proper flow of love from YHWH to man to woman, there is a similar need for structural flow among the members of the assembly. Brotherhood and sisterhood are not just about encouragement, they are about reinforcing divine alignment.

This structure lays the groundwork for discussions on familial extension to be explored biblically in another space. For now, the principle remains: peer relationships must reflect the divine order, never subvert it.

Order First, Then Fellowship

Horizontal love is sustained by vertical order

Without headship, fellowship becomes chaotic. Without vertical alignment, horizontal interaction becomes selfish and fleshly. In the covenant community, love among peers is not sustained by feelings or preferences but by order. Men who walk in obedience to Messiah will treat their brothers with integrity. Women who submit to their husbands will treat their sisters with grace. When each part is submitted vertically, love overflows horizontally.

This is why Scripture constantly returns to structure before affection. Paul doesn’t begin his epistles with emotional language, he begins with order. It is only once believers are rightly ordered under Messiah that they are equipped to rightly love one another. The vertical comes first because the horizontal depends on it. One cannot have fellowship with others if they are not first in fellowship with YHWH through obedience.

Disruption of headship creates relational chaos

Where order is broken, chaos reigns. A man who rebels against Messiah will lead others into confusion. A woman who refuses submission will tear down her household and poison her sisters with rebellion. Even well-meaning believers will become toxic when their relationships are no longer grounded in proper headship.

This breakdown leads to hostel factions, gossip, resentment, and instability. What begins as a small act of rebellion against structure becomes a flood of dysfunction in the assembly. The loss of order means the loss of clarity, and without clarity, love deteriorates into manipulation. As Paul warned the Corinthians,

“Let all things be done decently and in order.”

That order is not optional, it is essential to relational peace. Order guards the heart of fellowship and sets boundaries so love can operate freely within.

In short, the assembly cannot flourish broadly unless it is first submitted vertically. And where headship is rejected, relationships suffer. Alignment is the soil in which fellowship grows.

Compassion as Overflow, Not Replacement

Charity, mercy, and emotional support have their place, but they are never substitutes for obedience. In our day, many use “love” as a tool to dismantle headship. They argue for compassion without correction, empathy without instruction, and unity without order. But Scripture offers no such foundation. True love does not bypass divine structure, it flows from it.

Compassion, when rightly ordered, is the fruit of a submitted life. It is the overflow of alignment, not a replacement for it. A man shows compassion to his brother because he walks in obedience to Messiah. A woman serves her sister because she is aligned with her husband. Compassion is not a license to affirm rebellion; it is a call to restore those who have fallen from order. It says, “I will love you enough to help you get back under your covering.”

This is why the widows were helped, the fatherless were protected, and the stranger was welcomed, but all within a framework that preserved and reinforced structure. Compassion in Scripture always affirms covenant, never undermines it. Charity without order is like blood outside the body, it may have nutrients, but it cannot give life.

In the end, love among peers is not horizontal sentiment, it is the overflow of vertical submission. It is love in order, for order, and through order. That is the only love YHWH blesses, when we allow our cup to overflow.

Reclaiming Love Through Headship and Submission

Headship Is the Divine Mechanism by Which Love Is Administered

True love is not random, emotional, or instinctual, it is ordered, initiated, and administered through headship. The entirety of Scripture reveals this: YHWH loved by initiating covenant, by setting expectations, by commanding obedience, and by offering Himself in faithfulness to a wayward people. His love is not chaotic; it is structured, sacrificial, and supremely sovereign.

That same model is echoed in Messiah’s relationship to man. Yeshua initiates with love, intercedes with patience, and governs with mercy. He does not wait for man to be deserving. He moves first, and man must respond. Headship is thereby, not a privilege for man to exploit, but a sacred mechanism through which love flows from heaven into every home, from every man into every member under his covering.

When headship is rightly understood, love becomes more than a feeling, it becomes a holy conduit. It is the divine pipeline through which order brings life, correction brings safety, and authority brings peace. To remove headship is to remove the foundation on which love is delivered. Without it, love becomes sentiment divorced from structure, incapable of sustaining covenant, inadequate to protect from harm.

Disregarding Authority Structure = Rebellion Against YHWH’s Design

To ignore or challenge the divine hierarchy is not a matter of preference, it is rebellion. From the garden of Eden to the rebellion of Korah, Scripture is replete with warnings against the rejection of structure. Adam allowed Eve to overstep her bounds. Korah presumed equality with Moses. Miriam spoke out of turn against the authority of Moses’ marriage. In every case, rebellion against authority was rebellion against YHWH Himself.

In modern terms, rejecting the authority of Messiah is now seen as freedom in worship. Yet that "freedom" leads to lawlessness. So too, when a woman rejects the authority of her husband, she is not exercising independence, she is tearing down the house YHWH ordained her to help build. This is not merely a domestic issue; it is cosmic treason.

YHWH's design is not negotiable. He did not ask for opinions when establishing order. To challenge the structure is to side with the enemy, who said,

“I will ascend… I will make myself like the Most High” (Isaiah 14:13–14).

Whether man rejects Messiah or woman rejects her husband, the spirit is the same: insubordination masked as autonomy. Rebellion is the spirit of Asherah, the spirit of Jezebel, it is the spirit of the Queen of Heaven, the elevation of the feminine submissive into an autonomous place of masculine headship, making the usurper, male or female, a replacement for Elohim. And a poor replacement we make.

When men reject their calling to sacrificially lead, they commit passive rebellion. When women reject their calling to reverently submit, they commit active rebellion. Both break the circuit of divine love and bring death to the household. Order is not optional; it is sacred.

Submission & Authority—Not Optional, They Are Sacred Assignments

Obedience in the divine order is not based on understanding, it is based on faith. Abraham was not given an explanation when told to sacrifice Isaac. His obedience was not delayed by confusion or held hostage to emotion. He rose early and obeyed, trusting the nature of YHWH even when the instruction seemed unthinkable.

So too, a wife is not commanded to submit only when her husband makes sense to her or agrees with her. Her role is to trust the structure; not because the man is infallible, but because YHWH is. Just as Abraham trusted YHWH, so a woman must trust YHWH’s appointed structure, even when it seems inconsistent to her. She is not her husband's judge, she is his helper. Her silence and submission become her worship, and her reverence becomes the sweet aroma that rises before YHWH. If the man abuses that, his Head will handle him, and it won't go well for him.

1 Peter 3:1–2 confirms this. A wife wins over her husband not with many words, arguments, or resistance, but with respectful and pure conduct. This is not weakness; this is warfare against the spirit of rebellion. Just as Messiah submitted to the Father even unto death, so too is the woman called to submit to her husband as unto the Master.

Sarah, the covenantal standard of femininity, submitted to Abraham to the point of calling him “master.” She did not withhold obedience based on circumstances. She yielded, honored, and aligned. And Peter tells us that this is the model for all righteous women; those who hope in YHWH. The instruction is not given for our modern culture, it is given for His Kingdom, and the culture of that Kingdom that we must assimilate into.

Likewise, men are not permitted to set aside their responsibility to lead when life becomes difficult or when their wives push back. A man must remain submitted to Messiah regardless of how misunderstood, dishonored, or weary he feels. He is the image of YHWH to his household, and his authority is a reflection of heaven. While it is true that he cannot lead what will not follow, his refusal to lead is even more dangerous than her refusal to follow, for can she follow what won't lead? Submission and authority are not mutually exclusive, they are mutually binding, sacred, and accountable.

Harmony, Peace, and Love Depend on Reestablishment of This Order

The world cries out for peace but refuses the structure that creates it. Harmony does not come from compromise, it comes from alignment. Love does not thrive in a vacuum, it thrives in order. Families, assemblies, and entire nations suffer not because they lack affection, but because they lack submission. The feminist chaos in culture is not a crisis of love, it is a crisis of headship. Effeminate rebellion is the blood drunk woman riding the beast of Revelation. Come out from among the chaos of the harlot, and submit to the household of YHWH.

To restore love, we must restore the circuit. The man must submit to Messiah and receive his mission. The woman must submit to her husband and receive her security. The children must submit to their parents and receive their instruction. The assembly of men must mutually submit to each other in cooperation and revive its direction. The whole body must submit to Messiah, its Head, and only then will peace reign.

When each part embraces its design, the system flows. YHWH's love pours down, authority is exercised in righteousness, submission rises in reverence, and the household becomes a dwelling place for His presence. This is the only path to shalom, not a feeling of peace, but the reality of it: wholeness, order, and blessing.

This is not a political agenda. Patriarchy is not a cultural movement. It is the heartbeat of heaven manifest in the earth. It is love as YHWH intended; ordered, reverent, and full of life. The restoration of love begins not with a feeling, but with repentance. We must repent for where we have inverted the structure, where we have made Messiah a servant to man, where man has become a servant to woman, and where woman has become her own head or the head of others.

Reformation begins in the home. Redemption begins with order. Love begins with submission to the design of the ultimate Patriarch.

Let the man rise and lead. Let the woman submit and build. Let the children obey and flourish. Let the body align, and the blessing will return. For in the reestablishment of divine headship, we do not only recover peace, we recover the image of YHWH upon the earth.
